# Commercial/Retail AI-Powered Stock Prediction Products: Accuracy Claims vs. Independent Verification

## What does EquBot claim about AIEQ's AI stock-picking accuracy, and how has the ETF actually performed vs. the S&P 500 since inception?

### Takeaway
EquBot does not publish a hard "accuracy percentage" for AIEQ's AI; its public claims are narrative (e.g., CIO statements that the fund "beat the benchmark" in certain years) rather than audited statistics. The ETF's actual, independently-verifiable investment performance (via Morningstar/Bloomberg/Yahoo-sourced return data) shows a mixed-to-negative record versus the S&P 500/total-market benchmarks over most multi-year windows since its October 2017 inception, contradicting the more optimistic company narrative.

### Cited Findings
- EquBot markets AIEQ as "the first and only actively managed ETF to fully utilize artificial intelligence... as a method for stock selection," built on IBM Watson, scanning millions of data points (filings, news, management profiles, sentiment, financials) to select roughly 30–70 (sometimes cited as 30–200) holdings — a **self-reported, promotional description**, not an accuracy statistic. — [CXO Advisory](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/fundamental-valuation/online-real-time-test-of-ai-stock-picking/)
- EquBot's own prospectus-level disclosure (per CXO Advisory's summary) warns that the underlying EquBot Model "may not be effective in finding stocks that outperform" over short horizons — i.e., **EquBot itself caveats the predictive claim**. — [CXO Advisory](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/fundamental-valuation/online-real-time-test-of-ai-stock-picking/)
- EquBot CIO Chris Natividad, in an April 2021 Wall Street Transcript interview, said: the fund "slightly underperformed" in year one, then "beat our benchmark" in year two, with the performance spread "continu[ing] to improve" into year three — a **self-reported, unaudited company narrative**, not a verified statistic. — [Wall Street Transcript via TWST](https://twst.com/?p=1040257)
- This narrative is contradicted by other independent reporting: Motley Fool reported that in 2018 (AIEQ's second partial/full year), the S&P 500 lost about 6% while AIEQ lost nearly 16% — the opposite of "beating the benchmark" in year two. — [Motley Fool / Nasdaq syndication](https://www.nasdaq.com/articles/ai-powered-etf-failed-miserably-beating-market-2018-heres-what-you-can-learn-its-mistakes)
- Independent, SEC-regulated fund performance data (via Yahoo Finance, citing Morningstar) showed: 3-year annualized return of 4.8% for AIEQ vs. 9.1% for VTI (Vanguard Total Stock Market ETF); 5-year annualized return of 5.5% for AIEQ vs. 8.6% for VTI. — [Yahoo Finance](https://money.yahoo.com/move-over-chatgpt-ai-powered-221500544.html)
- A Bloomberg-sourced report: AIEQ delivered ~64% total return since its October 2017 inception vs. ~75% for the S&P 500 Total Return Index (snapshot, exact date unclear from secondary source). — [Wealth Management / Bloomberg](https://www.wealthmanagement.com/etfs/ai-fund-that-was-trouncing-market-is-derailed-by-tech-bets)
- A January 2022 retrospective (almost five years post-launch): AIEQ up 57% since inception vs. 84% for the S&P 500 Total Return Index over the same span. — [Tech Brew](https://www.techbrew.com/stories/2022/01/26/how-the-world-s-first-ai-managed-etf-stacks-up-almost-five-years-later)
- The same retrospective notes a brief period of outperformance: AIEQ first underperformed, then matched, then "surpassed" the U.S. market in 2020, beating the S&P 500 by "at least seven percentage points" that year. — [Tech Brew](https://www.techbrew.com/stories/2022/01/26/how-the-world-s-first-ai-managed-etf-stacks-up-almost-five-years-later)
- Bloomberg's October 2018 coverage ("Robot ETF Leaves Pros in Dust") reported a period of strong early performance, including sharp calls on small-cap movers — reported as a snapshot, not a durable track record. — [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2018-10-19/inside-the-robot-mind-an-etf-nails-big-calls-to-match-market)
- Seeking Alpha contributors have repeatedly criticized the fund: one argued AIEQ "has underperformed against all major market segments since its launch in 2017"; a later Seeking Alpha piece ("AIEQ: An Example Of AI Failure") and another ("Mobilizing AI Resources For Disappointing Results") cite "lackluster returns, high volatility, high fees and excessive turnover." — [Seeking Alpha](https://seekingalpha.com/article/4761215-aieq-example-of-ai-failure); [Seeking Alpha](https://seekingalpha.com/article/4702788-aieq-mobilizing-ai-resources-for-disappointing-results)
- ETF Trends reported a disappointing one-year return of -13.92% at one point, stating "the AI technology underlying its investment decisions is not working as intended." — [ETF Trends](https://www.etftrends.com/?p=520600)
- Countering the negative narrative, Business Insider reported a strong stretch where AIEQ's 1-year return was 50.1% vs. 39.9% for a comparison index — though the same piece showed a weaker 3-month return, illustrating that short windows are cherry-picked by both critics and boosters. — [Business Insider](https://www.businessinsider.nl/an-artificial-intelligence-powered-etf-has-smashed-the-sp-500-in-the-last-month-without-any-meme-stocks/)
- A Motley Fool piece from October 2025 stated AIEQ "has lagged the S&P 500 since inception," has higher volatility, a high expense fee, and an "extremely high turnover rate." — [Motley Fool, via Advisor Perspectives](https://www.advisorperspectives.com/commentaries/2023/06/05/aieq-etf-implications-ai-investing)
- A comparison tool (undated) showed AIEQ's longest tracked common window (9 years) at +9.73% annualized vs. +14.18% annualized for SPY. — [ETF comparison tool, exact source unclear]
- As of the most current data gathered (Oct. 22, 2026, via TotalRealReturns, dividends reinvested): AIEQ +8.79% trailing 1-year, +8.75% YTD; since inception (Oct. 18, 2017) +125.10% nominal total return (~+9.42%/yr annualized). A separate tracker (Dividend Vision, updated Oct. 16 2026) showed a trailing 1-year total return of 10.8%. These two contemporary sources disagree by ~2 points, likely due to differing as-of dates/methodology — neither explicitly benchmarks against SPY/S&P 500 in the snippet retrieved. — [TotalRealReturns](https://totalrealreturns.com/n/AIEQ); [Dividend Vision](https://www.dividendvision.com/assets/tickers?ticker=AIEQ)
- An academic/independent research site, CXO Advisory, ran its own real-time, long-horizon comparison of AIEQ vs. SPY from inception (Oct 18, 2017) through at least September 2023, and concluded (per its site's broader summary page) that "very limited available evidence offers little support for belief that an AI-based equity fund can beat the market." This is the closest thing found to an independent, non-vendor-funded quantitative assessment. — [CXO Advisory](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/?p=30628); [Seeking Alpha methodology description](https://seekingalpha.com/article/4848715-aieq-the-human-vs-ai-stock-picking-test)

### Inferences
- AIEQ is unusual among the products in this research in that it has a genuine, independently-auditable track record (daily NAV, SEC-registered fund, third-party data via Morningstar/Bloomberg/Yahoo) — unlike the other products below, which mostly offer internal, unaudited "accuracy" metrics. This makes AIEQ the one case where the underlying investment outcome (not an internal "accuracy %") can be verified by anyone.
- That independently verifiable record shows AIEQ has, on balance across most multi-year windows examined (3-year, 5-year, ~5-year, ~9-year, since-inception as of 2021-2023), underperformed simple market-cap benchmarks (S&P 500 Total Return, VTI, SPY), with isolated periods (2020, mid-2018 small-cap calls, certain 1-year windows cited by bulls) of outperformance that get highlighted by both the company and friendly press.
- EquBot's own public statements about "beating the benchmark" are self-reported and, in at least one instance identified here, appear to conflict with contemporaneous independent reporting about the same period — reinforcing that company narrative should not be treated as equivalent to the audited return data.

### Gaps
- Could not retrieve the full CXO Advisory / Seeking Alpha "AIEQ: The Human Vs. AI Stock-Picking Test" results (paywalled) — the specific side-by-side performance conclusion from that comparison was not accessible.
- No current (2026) Morningstar star-rating or official Morningstar-published trailing-return table was directly retrieved (search results surfaced trackers citing Morningstar, but not a Morningstar page itself); the two 2026 trackers used (TotalRealReturns, Dividend Vision) disagree on the exact trailing-year number and neither gave a direct side-by-side S&P 500 comparison for the current date — a reader wanting the precise current gap should pull Morningstar's or the fund's own fact sheet directly.
- Could not confirm whether EquBot has ever published a formal "accuracy rate" (e.g., "X% of our picks outperformed") anywhere in its marketing — the claims found were narrative/qualitative ("beat our benchmark") rather than a quantified accuracy percentage.

## What accuracy/"K Score" claims does Kavout make, and is there independent verification?

### Takeaway
Kavout's accuracy claims rest entirely on its own published backtests ("K Score" historical performance tables), which it calls an "audit" but which is self-conducted and self-published, not performed or verified by an unaffiliated third party; no independent replication was found, and Kavout's own backtest numbers do not uniformly support its "outperformance" claim.

### Cited Findings
- Kavout's "Stock Wellness Check" marketing page claims a portfolio built from K Scores of 9 (its "Top Picks Portfolio") has "out-performed the S&P 500" — a **self-reported claim**. — [Kavout](https://www.kavout.com/stockwellnesscheck)
- Kavout's "K Score – US – Top 500 Stocks by Market Cap" product page states that a K Score of 7–9 signals "a higher probability of outperformance" — again **self-reported**, framed as probability rather than guaranteed accuracy. — [Kavout](https://kavout.com/product/kscore-us-top500-stocks-by-market-cap)
- On that same page, Kavout's own published backtest for 2012–2024 reports a Top Picks portfolio CAGR of 12.94% vs. 14.93% for SPY — i.e., alpha of **-1.54%**, meaning Kavout's own self-published numbers show its top-rated US large-cap picks underperforming the S&P 500 benchmark over that stretch, which runs counter to the "outperformance" marketing claim made elsewhere on the site. — [Kavout](https://kavout.com/product/kscore-us-top500-stocks-by-market-cap)
- Kavout's China-market K Score page shows a higher CAGR for its Top Picks portfolio there, but this is presented as a backtest, not a live/audited track record. — [Kavout](https://www.kavout.com/k-score-china/)
- A third-party comparison/guide site (Walnut Invest) states generally that when a stock-scoring tool "advertises a backtested win rate or an accuracy percentage, that number is its own, usually drawn from historical data on which the model was tuned," and argues no stock-scoring tool reliably predicts prices — this is commentary/opinion, not an independent empirical test of Kavout specifically, and the same source inconsistently refers to the product as "Kai Score." — [Walnut Invest](https://walnutinvest.com/resources/kavout-alternatives)
- Kavout's own disclaimers state its information "does not constitute investment advice" and that "actual alpha results may vary" — i.e., Kavout itself flags the backtest as not predictive of future results. — [Kavout](https://kavout.com/product/kscore-us-top500-stocks-by-market-cap)

### Inferences
- There is no evidence of an unaffiliated, outside party (academic researcher, regulator, independent journalist) auditing or replicating Kavout's K Score performance; every performance number traced back to Kavout's own website.
- The one concrete backtest figure available (12.94% vs 14.93% CAGR, 2012–2024 for the US top-500 "Top Picks" product) actually shows underperformance versus SPY, undercutting the plain-language "has out-performed the S&P 500" marketing claim found elsewhere on Kavout's own site — a notable internal inconsistency in the company's own public material.

### Gaps
- Could not find any academic paper, financial-journalism investigation, or regulator finding that independently tests K Score's forward predictive accuracy.
- Could not determine whether the "out-performed the S&P 500" claim and the "12.94% vs 14.93%" backtest refer to the same product/time period/universe, or whether they are different products (e.g., different K Score universes) — this inconsistency could not be resolved from available pages.

## What win-rate or accuracy claims does Trade Ideas make for Holly AI, and has any independent third party tested/verified these?

### Takeaway
Trade Ideas advertises win rates in the "low-to-mid sixties percent" and claims Holly has "consistently outperformed market benchmarks," but these are vendor-published figures (including same-day tallies posted on its own site, e.g. "5 wins out of 7 trades") with no disclosed methodology distinguishing live vs. simulated/backtested results; independent reviewers report live, real-money user results coming in lower than advertised, and no independent audit of the win-rate claim was found.

### Cited Findings
- Trade Ideas' own "What is Holly AI" learning-center page frames the product cautiously, noting that "even carefully searched strategies have win rates and payoff distributions" — i.e., not claiming 100% accuracy. — [Trade Ideas](https://www.trade-ideas.com/learning-center/ai-in-trading/what-is-holly-ai/)
- Trade Ideas' own "AI Trading Results" / Holly guide page displays a same-day, in-progress tally: "Today, Holly AI, so far, scored 5 wins out of 7 trades" (~71% for that one day) — presented without any disclosure of whether this reflects live, simulated, or backtested trades, and explicitly caveated as a same-day snapshot ("so far") that could change. — [Trade Ideas](https://www.trade-ideas.com/hollyguide/AI_Trading_Results/The_Trade_Ideas_AI_Experience_with_Holly.html)
- The same page states Holly uses "over 60 tailored algorithms" and generates "anywhere between 5 to 25 trades a day" — descriptive marketing claims, not accuracy statistics. — [Trade Ideas](https://www.trade-ideas.com/hollyguide/AI_Trading_Results/The_Trade_Ideas_AI_Experience_with_Holly.html)
- An independent review site reports that "Trade Ideas advertises win rates in the low-to-mid sixties percent, but users report that live, real-money results come in lower, and hard proof of actual profit is scarce." — [Liberated Stock Trader (via search synthesis)](https://www.liberatedstocktrader.com/trade-ideas-review/)
- A forum post (NexusFi) repeats a claimed 60% win rate with a 2:1 reward-to-risk ratio — but this is a user relaying a vendor claim, not independently verified data. — [NexusFi](https://nexusfi.com/showthread.php?p=845914)
- Finmasters reports the company's claim that "Holly has consistently outperformed market benchmarks." A 2017 Benzinga piece (citing a Trade Ideas press release) states returns came in "around 22 percent post-commissions" for a specific period. Both are vendor-sourced. — [Finmasters](https://finmasters.com/trade-ideas-review/); [Benzinga](https://feeds2.benzinga.com/fintech/17/07/9769334/meet-holly-the-trade-ideas-ai-analyst-that-beat-the-market)
- Some affiliate/coupon review pages claim Trade Ideas has "an audited track record of beating the market" — no audit documentation or auditor was identified, and these pages have a financial (commission) interest in positive framing, making them unreliable as independent verification. — [Liberated Stock Trader coupon page](https://liberatedstocktrader.com/trade-ideas-discount-coupon/)
- Nasdaq hosted contemporaneous "Trade Ideas Monthly Summary" results in 2020 (e.g., August, September 2020), which are checkable, dated, vendor-submitted performance recaps — one month showed a decline of -1.5%, indicating the published summaries include losing months, not only wins. — [Nasdaq](https://www.nasdaq.com/articles/trade-ideas-monthly-summary%3A-september-2020-2020-10-08)
- A 2026 roundup comparing 11 AI trading platforms explicitly declines to name a "best" platform based on vendor-reported win rates, implicitly flagging the unreliability of self-reported win-rate comparisons across the industry. — [FXStreet](https://www.fxstreet.com/press-releases/from-stock-selection-to-execution-comparing-11-ai-trading-platforms-in-2026-202608281437)

### Inferences
- No evidence surfaced of any named independent journalist, academic, or regulator having conducted a controlled, disclosed-methodology test of Holly AI's win rate; all specific win-rate/percentage figures trace back to Trade Ideas itself (website, press releases) or to affiliate marketing pages with a financial interest in the product.
- The gap between vendor-advertised win rates ("low-to-mid sixties percent") and user-reported live results (described by reviewers as "lower") is noted by independent reviewers but not quantified with hard data in the sources found.

### Gaps
- Could not find a rigorous, methodologically transparent third-party backtest or live-paper-trading test of Holly AI's actual win rate.
- Could not access the "Holly Performance" page linked from the Trade Ideas site (not retrieved) or the Liberated Stock Trader "Tickeron Review"-style deep benchmark test for Trade Ideas specifically (a similar Tickeron lab test exists but returned HTTP 403 on fetch — see Tickeron section).

## What accuracy claims does Tickeron make for its AI trading bots/patterns, and is there independent verification?

### Takeaway
Tickeron publishes specific self-reported accuracy figures (e.g., 85–92% accuracy cited for short-interval trading agents) and claims a "fully audited track record of every stock Bot under management," but no evidence was found of an outside auditor; independent reviewer commentary explicitly flags the accuracy stats as "not independently audited," and mixed/negative reviews report inconsistent real-world pattern-recognition performance.

### Cited Findings
- A 2026 summary attributes an "85–92% accuracy" figure for Tickeron's short-interval trading agents directly to the company's own data, explicitly distinguishing it from outside testing. — [search-synthesized from Liberated Stock Trader / softwarefinder listings](https://www.liberatedstocktrader.com/tickeron-review/)
- One product listing states plainly that "self-reported accuracy stats are not independently audited" for Tickeron. — [Liberated Stock Trader](https://www.liberatedstocktrader.com/tickeron-review/)
- Liberated Stock Trader (an independent review site) reports that "Tickeron offers a fully audited track record of every stock Bot under management" — however, this is the reviewer relaying Tickeron's own claim rather than the reviewer independently verifying an audit; no auditor name, standard, or audit report was identified in the sources retrieved (direct fetch of the detailed "Tickeron Benchmark Lab Test" page returned HTTP 403 and could not be read). — [Liberated Stock Trader](https://www.liberatedstocktrader.com/tickeron-benchmark-lab-test/) (fetch blocked; claim relayed via search snippet only)
- A separate August 2026-dated review (WalletInvestor) titled "Tickeron Review: Are Pre-Built AI Trading Bots Reliable for Pattern Recognition?" concludes the answer is "mixed." — [WalletInvestor](https://walletinvestor.com/news/finance-news/tickeron-review-are-pre-built-ai-trading-bots-reliable-for-pattern-recognition/)
- Another reviewer lists "accuracy inconsistent on some pattern signals" as a drawback of the product. — [daytradingtoolkit.com](https://daytradingtoolkit.com/reviews/tickeron-review)
- Tickeron's pattern scanner is described (company/marketing material and press release) as covering roughly 39–40 chart patterns across stocks, ETFs, crypto, and forex, scanned in real time — a feature description, not an accuracy statistic. — [Barchart/press release](https://www.barchart.com/story/news/29025045/tickeron-revolutionizes-stock-pattern-discovery-with-ai-powered-automation-in-seconds)

### Inferences
- As with the other products, every specific accuracy percentage for Tickeron traces back to the company itself; the "fully audited" language used by at least one reviewer appears to be relaying Tickeron's own marketing claim rather than describing a verified outside audit — no auditor, audit firm, or audit report was identified anywhere in the sources found.
- Independent (non-paid) reviews that did test or comment on real-world usage describe results as "mixed" or "inconsistent," which is at odds with the high (85–92%) self-reported accuracy figures.

### Gaps
- Could not retrieve the full content of the "Liberated Stock Trader Tickeron Benchmark Lab Test" or "Tickeron Lab Test Review" pages (HTTP 403 on direct fetch) — these appeared from search snippets to be the closest thing to a hands-on independent test among the sources found, and a follow-up researcher with browser access could try to retrieve them directly for more detail.
- Could not identify who, specifically, performs the "audit" Tickeron/reviewers reference, or what standard it follows.

## Has any independent financial journalist, YouTuber, or researcher done a real test/review of any of these products' actual predictive accuracy? (Cross-cutting)

### Takeaway
Across all five products researched, genuinely independent, methodologically transparent, third-party-verified accuracy testing is essentially absent. The one partial exception is AIEQ, where independent financial data providers (Morningstar-sourced data via Yahoo Finance, Bloomberg reporting, CXO Advisory's own real-time tracking study) provide real, checkable investment-return data — but even there, EquBot's own narrative "accuracy"/"beat the benchmark" claims are separate from, and not always consistent with, that independently verifiable return data. For Kavout, Trade Ideas, Tickeron, Danelfin, I Know First, and Vantagepoint A.I., every specific accuracy/win-rate percentage found traces back to the company's own website, press release, or an affiliate/review site with a financial incentive (commissions) that relays — but does not independently verify — the vendor's claim.

### Cited Findings
- CXO Advisory (an evidence-based investing research site) ran its own long-horizon, real-time comparison of AIEQ vs. SPY and concluded broadly that "very limited available evidence offers little support for belief that an AI-based equity fund can beat the market" — the closest finding in this research to an independent, non-vendor-funded empirical assessment of any of these products. — [CXO Advisory](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/?p=30628)
- Seeking Alpha's "AIEQ: The Human Vs. AI Stock-Picking Test" is an independent author's side-by-side benchmark comparison (AIEQ vs. SPY, dividend-adjusted, Oct 2017–Sep 2023), but the specific results are paywalled and could not be retrieved. — [Seeking Alpha](https://seekingalpha.com/article/4848715-aieq-the-human-vs-ai-stock-picking-test)
- Vantagepoint A.I. (a separate retail AI trading-software vendor, not one of the four named in the original assignment but relevant context) issued a press release claiming "two comprehensive independent validation studies" by PhD researchers showing accuracy "up to 62.72% better than randomized trading" — this is a vendor press release describing studies it commissioned, so its independence is unverified from the source itself. — [press release, cited via search](https://benzinga.com/z/32545338) (exact press release URL not directly confirmed in this research pass)
- Danelfin's accuracy claim rests on a "provider-published AI Score audit" — explicitly described by a reviewer as "not the same as an independent live replication by an unaffiliated research team." — [Walnut Invest](https://walnutinvest.com/resources/danelfin-review)
- I Know First's widely-cited "97% accuracy" figure is a company claim relayed by Benzinga ("according to the company") with no independent audit identified; other promotional pages show cherry-picked individual-stock results (e.g., "81% in seven days") rather than full track records. — [Benzinga](https://benzinga.com/z/14482361)
- A secondary blog source (Lambdafin) cites academic-style direction-accuracy estimates for AI stock-prediction models generally around 49–54% — "near a coin flip" — but this is a secondary summary, not a primary peer-reviewed study, and was not tied to any specific named commercial product. — [Lambdafin](https://www.lambdafin.com/articles/ai-stock-prediction-accuracy)
- General commentary from an independent comparison guide (Walnut Invest) states plainly: "vendors choose which numbers to publish, over which windows, on which universe. Without independent verification, an accuracy figure is a marketing claim, not evidence" — a general critique applicable across this entire product category. — [Walnut Invest](https://walnutinvest.com/resources/most-accurate-ai-stock-predictor)

### Inferences
- The retail "AI stock prediction" product category, as a whole, lacks third-party, auditor-verified accuracy claims. The pattern across EquBot, Kavout, Trade Ideas, Tickeron, Danelfin, and I Know First is consistent: companies publish their own backtests or win-rate figures (sometimes labeling them "audited"), review/affiliate sites relay these claims (often while earning referral commissions), and no outside academic, regulatory, or journalistic body was found to have independently replicated or audited any of these specific figures.
- AIEQ is the one case offering a path to real independent verification — not because EquBot's internal "accuracy" claims are audited, but because the ETF itself is an SEC-registered, publicly traded fund whose actual investment returns are tracked by independent data providers (Morningstar, Bloomberg) regardless of what EquBot claims about its underlying AI.

### Gaps
- No independent journalist or YouTuber "live test" (e.g., paper-trading a platform for N months and publishing raw, falsifiable results) was identified for Kavout, Trade Ideas/Holly AI, or Tickeron in this research pass; review sites found (Liberated Stock Trader, WalletInvestor, Finmasters, daytradingtoolkit, Walnut Invest, beginnersinai) are a mix of hands-on reviewer commentary and affiliate marketing, and it was often unclear from available snippets which category a given claim fell into.
- Could not verify whether the "two independent validation studies" cited in Vantagepoint A.I.'s press release were conducted by genuinely unaffiliated researchers or were commissioned/funded by Vantagepoint itself — the underlying study documents were not located in this research pass.
- Time and tool-call budget constraints limited the number of named "other well-known retail AI stock-prediction products" investigated beyond Danelfin, I Know First, and Vantagepoint A.I. (mentioned briefly for context); a deeper pass could examine additional products (e.g., Composer, TrendSpider, Magnifi, Trefis) if needed.
