# Two viral AI trading windfalls debunked

Neither viral claim investigated here — @techluisenzo's "19-year-old Japanese student turned $68 into $750,000 with a Claude Code bot" and @FutureGirlAi's "Grok Bot Terminal turned $69.67 into $13,370 overnight" — has a shred of independent evidence behind it: no named person, no code repository, no exchange or wallet record, no press coverage, and in the Grok case no evidence the account or the product it describes even exists. Both fit, point-for-point, a documented and actively-monitored 2025-2026 fraud/engagement-farming template in which "AI built my trading bot overnight" is used as a brand-impersonation hook — a template blockchain-forensics firm TRM Labs has already traced to a real theft of **roughly $517,000 from 224 victims** using fake "Claude"-branded bot tutorials. Separately, a dedicated 2025-2026 literature survey found that nothing credible — no peer-reviewed paper combining above-66% accuracy with disclosed leakage-proof methodology, no hedge fund disclosing an audited hit rate, no Kaggle benchmark, no open-source agent framework — has verifiably cleared the previously-established 55-66% direction-accuracy ceiling; the field's most rigorous 2026 work is actively reinforcing, not overturning, that ceiling. For the engineering decision this report exists to inform, the good news is that the viral claims are noise to be ignored entirely, while the real, evidence-backed opportunity lies elsewhere: a concrete menu of accuracy techniques (judge-based aggregation over majority voting, hybrid generalist/specialist regimes, output-token-focused prompting) and latency techniques (output-format trimming, context caching, service-tier selection) that production case studies and controlled academic studies have already measured, several of which map directly onto this system's known weak points — its TSLA-like narrative-stock blind spot and its ~68-second, output-token-heavy three-call pipeline.

## The $68-to-$750,000 "Japanese student" story has no verifiable trace

The tweet itself is real and was located directly — "A 19-year-old Japanese student built a trading bot with Claude Code in 2 days... First night: $6,732 profit. Starting capital: $68. Total profit so far: $750,000" ([x.com/techluisenzo/status/2113607304776188067](https://x.com/techluisenzo/status/2113607304776188067)) — but everything underneath it dissolves on inspection. No independent search (by name, by the specific dollar figures, by "iPad as a second monitor," or by nationality-specific Japanese-language press) turned up any news article, forum thread, GitHub repository, exchange statement, or secondary retelling corroborating the student, the bot, or the numbers; the only verbatim match anywhere on the indexed web is the tweet itself. That absence is itself diagnostic: a genuine story of a teenager turning $68 into $750,000 would ordinarily generate follow-on coverage or skeptical scrutiny, the way a comparable "ChatGPT micro-cap portfolio" teen story did on Reddit with verifiable posted trades — and none of that exists here. The account posting the claim, "Luis Enzo" (@techluisenzo), has no identifiable real person behind it: no bio trail, no press mentions, and its own post history is a repeating viral-bait template — the same account posted a near-identical "overnight AI windfall" story days apart with different numbers and a different triggering misfortune ("I lost my job yesterday... $25 → $4,237 in one night" scanning Polymarket wallets) ([x.com/techluisenzo/status/2115344418337436104](https://x.com/techluisenzo/status/2115344418337436104)), alongside generic AI-productivity engagement bait (a "Claude rewrote my LinkedIn profile" thread, a Steve Jobs public-speaking thread). Two structurally identical extreme-overnight-multiplier stories from the same account in the same short window is strong internal evidence of a reusable content formula, not two independently reported real events.

The arithmetic itself is the second red flag: $68 → $6,732 overnight is roughly a **9,800% single-night gain**, and $68 → $750,000 cumulative is roughly a **1,100,000% total gain** — magnitudes achievable in real markets only through extreme leverage (which makes total loss far more common than extreme gain), through tiny illiquid markets prone to manipulation, through cherry-picking one winning trade out of many unreported losers, or through a fabricated/simulated dashboard. An independent writer who actually built and ran a comparable small-capital AI/Polymarket bot transparently reported the opposite outcome — the balance "fell from about $23 to roughly $1.50" ([dev.to](https://dev.to/rhecustein/is-the-50-2980-polymarket-ai-bot-real-i-built-one-to-find-out-n8k)) — and Decrypt's investigation of a similar viral Polymarket "AI bot" claim (5,860% in two days, 4.7M views) found the posting account was only five months old and monetizing via a referral link, not reporting a verified result ([Yahoo Finance/Decrypt](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/ai-trading-bots-booming-trust-150102860.html)). On the technical side, Claude Code genuinely can help write trading-bot code quickly — legitimate, independently documented examples exist of developers using it for crypto-trading tooling over dozens of iterative sessions ([dev.to: 94 sessions later](https://dev.to/cart0ne/i-used-claude-code-to-build-a-crypto-trading-bot-94-sessions-later-heres-what-works-1i2j)) — but writing code is categorically different from having live brokerage API keys, real funded capital, real-time data feeds across "50 markets simultaneously," and unattended overnight order-execution infrastructure, none of which the tweet shows or describes. "Used his iPad as a second monitor" is scene-setting narrative color, not a technical disclosure. A directly relevant precedent shows what "Claude built my trading bot" branding can actually mean in practice: TRM Labs' forensic investigation of a real "Claude"-branded arbitrage-bot scam found **no AI functionality in the underlying smart contracts at all** — the AI branding was "pure marketing" — in a campaign that stole roughly $517,000 from 224 victims ([airdropalert.com](https://airdropalert.com/blogs/claude-trading-bot-scam/); [cryptobriefing.com](https://cryptobriefing.com/trm-labs-fake-ai-bot-tutorial-scam/)). A broader investigative piece on this exact genre, "The AI Quant Bot Mirage," documents a cluster of accounts running identical templates — AI-branded quant-bot claims, implausible win rates, neon fake-dashboard screenshots, giveaway bait, and Telegram copytrade funnels — and states plainly that "Anthropic doesn't make trading bots" and that real Claude/Cursor users in this space build alert tools where humans still execute trades ([dev.to](https://dev.to/__747bb5a1521/the-ai-quant-bot-mirage-inside-the-polymarket-copytrade-scam-network-flooding-crypto-twitter-1856)). No fact-checking organization has covered this specific tweet by name, most likely because its reach (45K views) sits well below the threshold that typically draws formal fact-checker attention — but that is an explanation for the silence, not evidence the claim is true. **Verdict: no independent evidence found; self-reported and unverified; pattern-matches a documented fraud/engagement-farming template.**

## The "Grok Bot Terminal" $69.67-to-$13,370 claim fares no better

This claim is weaker on every axis than the first. No X/Twitter account matching the handle "@FutureGirlAi" or a person named "Jane Austen" tied to it could be located through any search angle — exact handle, name pairing, or associated project terms — meaning the account's existence itself, let alone its credibility, could not be confirmed in this research pass. The underlying system described — a six-module "SCOUT/FILTER/ANALYST/EXECUTOR/RISK/LOGGER" autonomous trading terminal called "sol-desk" running on "Grok" — has zero independent trace: no GitHub repository, no on-chain wallet, no news coverage, no forum discussion, and a search for the exact dollar figures "$69.67" and "$13,370" together returned **zero matching pages anywhere on the indexed web**. Of the five meme-coin tickers named in the claimed screenshot, only $ZAPCAT corresponds to a real (explicitly "unverified") Solana token; $PIPPA, $FIZZLE, and $KAPPU match no real tokens at all on trackers that index virtually every Pump.fun launch, and $WOBBL matches only unrelated low-cap tokens — strongly suggesting the dashboard's contents are mocked up or illustrative rather than a live trading terminal. xAI does have a real product called "Grok Bot" (launched roughly August 2026) supporting persistent agents and group chats of two to six bots ([xAI: Introducing Grok Bot](https://x.ai/news/introducing-grok-bot)) — likely the source of the "six agents" framing — but it is a general-purpose office-automation product, not a trading system, and no project named "sol-desk" or matching the claimed module pipeline exists on xAI's own third-party bot marketplace or anywhere else searched. This is a recognizable misinformation technique: borrowing a kernel of real product terminology to lend credibility to an invented, specific claim.

The math checks out as stated ($13,370 / $69.67 ≈ 191.9x, roughly a **19,100% overnight gain**), and this is not mathematically impossible in isolation — Solana meme-coins are high-variance enough that individual trades occasionally produce triple-digit multiples — but no independently verified autonomous-trading result anywhere near this magnitude was found anywhere in this research; the closest analogous self-reported results found were far smaller and often negative (a $500 bot falling to $280; a $33 bot netting $0.80-$1.60 per month) ([dev.to](https://dev.to/maymay5692/running-a-crypto-bot-on-33-the-honest-math-40hk)). Industry reporting on this exact genre explicitly flags the "$X by morning" screenshot format as inherently unverifiable, since dashboard/balance screenshots are trivially easy to fabricate or stage ([InsiderLLM](https://insiderllm.com/guides/openclaw-trading-scams/)), and ZachXBT's documented investigations of crypto-Twitter manipulation networks describe exactly this combination — AI-generated personas, synchronized engagement, "screenshots of screenshots" — as a deliberate trust-building tactic used before pivoting an audience toward a monetized scam or token promotion ([OneKey summary](https://onekey.so/blog/ecosystem/zachxbt-exposes-a-social-media-manipulation-network-fake-war-news-used-to-funnel-traffic-into-crypto-scams-worth-hundreds-of-thousands-20260323174227)). The narrative details in the claim itself — "I never touched a trade," "put the phone on charge and went to bed," precise-to-the-cent dollar figures, jargon like "hard handoffs" — are the same engagement-optimized storytelling shape documented across these known scam and hype patterns, independent of whether this specific post is a scam funnel, pure engagement bait, or an embellished retelling of one lucky trade. **Verdict: no independent evidence found for the account, the product, or the result; the claim cannot be distinguished from a fabricated or staged screenshot.**

## Both claims fit a documented, quantified fraud template — this is not a one-off judgment call

What elevates this from "two suspicious tweets" to an engineering-relevant conclusion is that the pattern they fit has already been independently documented, named, and in one case criminally quantified. TRM Labs' Sept 2026 investigation found fake "Claude"-branded AI arbitrage-bot tutorials — explicitly promising autonomous, hands-off overnight gains, the identical hook used in both viral claims here — got **224 victims** to deploy bots with no real trading logic, stealing roughly **$517,000** ([TRM Labs](https://www.trmlabs.com/resources/blog/fake-ai-trading-bots-are-getting-victims-to-build-their-own-drainers); [cryptobriefing.com](https://cryptobriefing.com/trm-labs-fake-ai-bot-tutorial-scam/)). The "AI Quant Bot Mirage" investigation independently catalogues the same account-network behavior across dozens of similar posts, including internally impossible figures (a 51% win rate claimed alongside a 2,140% return — mathematically inconsistent without ruinous leverage — and a screenshot showing more BTC than exists in total circulating supply) ([dev.to](https://dev.to/__747bb5a1521/the-ai-quant-bot-mirage-inside-the-polymarket-copytrade-scam-network-flooding-crypto-twitter-1856)). A broader, non-AI-specific data point reinforces the prior: daytrading.com's analysis of viral investing tweets generally found **61% of trade calls result in losses** ([daytrading.com](https://www.daytrading.com/about-us/media/x)) — even setting the "AI" framing aside, viral trading claims on social media are predominantly unreliable. For an engineering and business decision, the operative conclusion is simple: neither claim should be treated as a benchmark, a competitive threat, or any kind of evidence about what Claude Code, Grok, or any LLM-driven trading system can actually do with live capital. They are marketing-shaped engagement content (at best) or active fraud lures (at the documented worst), and the correct response is to disregard them entirely rather than attempt to match or exceed their claimed numbers.

## No credible 2025-2026 system has verifiably cleared the 55-66% ceiling

A dedicated survey of 2025-2026 research, hedge-fund disclosures, Kaggle benchmarks, and open-source trading-agent frameworks found that the previously-established 55-66% directional-accuracy ceiling for genuinely out-of-sample stock-direction prediction still holds — nothing found combines above-66% accuracy, full peer review, and disclosed leakage-proof methodology all at once. The closest candidates each miss a leg: a 2025 PLOS Complex Systems paper claims **82% directional accuracy on SPY** using a volatility-regime-switching DLSTM, but its abstract discloses no train/test split or leakage-control detail, leaving its methodology unverifiable from available text ([IDEAS/RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/plo/pcsy00/0000037.html)); a University of Ottawa arXiv preprint reports **72% one-day directional accuracy** with genuinely disclosed leakage controls (chronological splits, training-data-only normalization, causal attention masking) but uses only 20 stocks, a single fixed split rather than walk-forward testing, and is explicitly unreviewed, merely "submitted" to a journal ([arXiv 2603.19136](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.19136v2)). A sister paper from the same research group that *is* published in peer-reviewed IEEE Access reports **65%** — squarely inside, not above, the established band ([arXiv 2603.05917](https://ideas.repec.org/p/arx/papers/2603.05917.html)). As a sobering counterpoint, general-purpose time-series foundation models (Chronos, TimesFM) tested on finance score **barely above 51%** ([arXiv 2511.18578](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.18578v1)), and a 92%+ figure from a low-selectivity conference proceedings paper is flagged as a near-certain leakage artifact consistent with the "Profit Mirage" pattern already established in prior research ([Atlantis Press](https://www.atlantis-press.com/proceedings/iciaai-25/126015330)).

No named hedge fund, prop firm, or fintech was found to have disclosed an audited *directional hit-rate* figure in 2025-2026 — the one fund claiming an "audit" (Vertus, 51.15% 2025 return, 2.13 Sharpe) was audited only on returns and Sharpe ratio, never on hit rate ([ETF.com](https://www.etf.com/sections/news/vertus-achieves-1-billion-daily-trading-milestone-closes-2025-51-returns)), and newer firms (AlphaGainium, SupraQuant) received coverage built entirely around AUM, volume, and trust-marketing narratives rather than disclosed predictive methodology. No Kaggle competition (Hull Tactical - Market Prediction, Martingale Hacks) produced a verifiable winning accuracy figure — these competitions score on correlation/utility metrics rather than a clean hit-rate percentage, itself a telling choice given how unflattering a transparent accuracy number would likely be. TauricResearch's widely-cited open-source multi-agent framework TradingAgents has published no updated accuracy figures since its original 2024 paper; its 2025-2026 changelog is dominated instead by "look-ahead and point-in-time fixes" to its backtesting pipeline ([GitHub](https://github.com/TauricResearch/TradingAgents)) — an implicit admission that earlier headline numbers were contaminated by the exact leakage problem this report's earlier research already named. Most tellingly, the field's newest and most careful 2025-2026 work is actively extending the memorization-leakage finding rather than overturning it: a CUHK Business School paper built a "Lookahead-Avoidance Probability" measure and found that the memorization-linked component of an LLM's apparent predictive edge **loses statistical significance entirely on samples drawn after the model's training cutoff** ([arXiv 2512.23847](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.23847v1)), and a follow-on mitigation paper, MemGuard-Alpha, is now building defensive tooling to filter memorization-contaminated signals out of trading strategies before they cause harm ([arXiv 2603.26797](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.26797.pdf)) — evidence the field is now managing this problem, not claiming to have solved it. The single most rigorous *genuinely live, forward-tested* 2026 result found (Chen & Pu's "Agentic AI nowcasting," collecting real-time predictions daily from April 2025 using live web search rather than curated text) reports a real but narrow edge — 18.4 bps/day alpha and 2.43 annualized Sharpe longing the top-20 ranked Russell 1000 stocks, with no edge in the bottom tier and alpha diluting quickly beyond the top ranks ([arXiv 2601.11958](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.11958)) — but this is an alpha/Sharpe claim, not a direction-accuracy percentage, and cannot be used to claim the ceiling has been cleared. **The honest 2025-2026 verdict is unchanged from the prior established baseline: the 55-66% ceiling still stands, and the field's trajectory is toward more skepticism about inflated claims, not toward a credible breakthrough above it.**

## Concrete, evidence-backed levers for our own system

Two separate research passes targeted techniques that could realistically move this system's own numbers — ~34% exact 3-way decision accuracy, ~54% overall direction accuracy (up to 81% on SPY), and ~68-second average latency on `gemini-3.5-flash-lite` — and both converged on a small number of well-evidenced, high-leverage changes plus several commonly-proposed ideas that the evidence does not actually support.

On accuracy, the single most actionable and best-evidenced finding directly challenges this system's current three-call majority-vote architecture: "When Agents Disagree: The Selection Bottleneck" (arXiv 2603.20324), a controlled 42-task, N=210 study comparing aggregation methods over a diverse model team, found **majority voting scored statistically equivalent to a single model** (Bradley-Terry win rate 0.496, vs. 0.500 parity) while **judge-based selection scored dramatically higher** (0.810) — meaning model/framing diversity under a vote is "inert," and the aggregation method, not the diversity itself, is what drives quality ([alphaxiv.org/abs/2603.20324](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2603.20324.md)). This directly implies that if the current 3-parallel-call-majority-vote pipeline behaves like the paper's "vote" condition, replacing majority voting with an LLM-judge or rule-based arbiter that selects the single best of the three outputs is likely a higher-leverage change than adopting a Bull/Bear debate mechanism — no study anywhere found provides a controlled, same-task comparison of debate vs. independent voting specifically for stock-direction prediction, so debate should be treated as unproven, not dismissed, pending the team's own A/B test. On segmentation, the most rigorous long-horizon study found (Hanauer et al., U.S. stock returns 1957-2023) showed a **hybrid of generalist-plus-specialist models outperformed either pure approach alone** out-of-sample ([Quantpedia](https://quantpedia.com/are-sector-specific-machine-learning-models-better-than-generalists/)) — for a system already showing an 81%-vs-54% gap between broad ETFs and narrative stocks like TSLA, this suggests segmenting along a volatility/narrative-dependence axis (with a specialist adjustment layered on a generalist base, not a pure TSLA-specific model prone to regime overfitting) is better-supported than sector-based segmentation. On prompting, self-consistency (many samples of one model, majority-voted) has a strong, well-quantified general-reasoning gain (+17.9 percentage points on GSM8K, Wang et al. 2022) but its marginal value has reportedly shrunk on stronger modern models ([arXiv 2511.00751](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.00751)) and no dedicated stock-prediction study was found — it should be measured empirically on the actual Gemini models in use, not assumed. Calibration research delivers an important caution directly relevant to this system's existing `confidence_calibration_map.json`: reasoning-enhanced models do **not** show better calibration than simpler ones even at comparable accuracy, and models average a 27% error rate even when expressing >90% confidence ([arXiv 2512.16030](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.16030v1)) — validating and continuing to invest in post-hoc calibration mapping (rather than expecting better prompting alone to fix overconfidence) is well-supported by this literature. On the TSLA-style narrative-stock weak spot specifically, naive sentiment aggregation is not a reliable fix: a TSLA-specific FinBERT+LSTM sentiment study scored 70.6% in training but only **46%** in a 63-day live paper-trading simulation — worse than a coin flip out of sample ([academic preprint via search]) — while a separate 2025 hybrid model found sentiment helps narrative/high-discourse stocks (+21.6% average accuracy improvement) but actively hurts low-discourse stocks, implying any sentiment integration needs to be event-triggered and discourse-aware rather than a blanket daily-aggregate feature. Finally, an LLM-qualitative-output-plus-gradient-boosting ensemble — a very natural extension for this system — has **no direct supporting study anywhere found**; the closest analogue (LSTM+LightGBM+CatBoost stacking, 10-15% improvement, arXiv 2505.23084) is suggestive but this specific hybrid remains a genuine, unvalidated white space that would need in-house experimentation.

On latency, the evidence points overwhelmingly at one lever: **output-token reduction**, not input compression, model choice, or call count. A production case study at incident.io cut a comparable structured-output LLM pipeline from 11 seconds to under 2.3 seconds (a ~79% reduction) almost entirely by trimming output verbosity — removing an optional free-text "reasoning" field cut output tokens 315→170 and latency 11s→7s, and switching to a compact output format cut output tokens a further ~70% and brought latency under 2.3s — versus only a ~20% latency gain from an 80%+ input-token cut via a denser input format ([incident.io](https://incident.io/building-with-ai/optimizing-llm-prompts)). Their stated principle — output tokens cost roughly 4x more in latency than input tokens, and latency scales roughly linearly with output length — directly implies that each of this system's three Gemini calls should return a compact structured verdict plus confidence and minimal rationale rather than a long prose analysis of all 100+ signals; this is likely the single biggest available win given the system's known output-heavy call pattern. Context caching of the shared prompt scaffold (schema, scoring rubric, few-shot examples) across the three parallel calls is officially supported on `gemini-3.5-flash-lite` and should reduce time-to-first-token, though Google publishes no quantitative benchmark for the effect ([Vertex AI context cache overview](https://docs.cloud.google.com/vertex-ai/generative-ai/docs/context-cache/context-cache-overview)). Because `gemini-3.5-flash-lite` already defaults to Google's lowest "minimal" thinking level, there is no further thinking-budget latency to recover via configuration — the 68-second baseline is not a thinking-overhead problem, reinforcing that output/input token volume is the real bottleneck. Google's synchronous "Priority" service tier, documented at "seconds" latency versus Standard's "seconds to minutes" (at a 75-100% cost premium), is a direct, quality-neutral lever worth load-testing against the current baseline ([ai.google.dev optimization guide](https://aistudio.google.com/docs/optimization)). One architectural question the team may be tempted to "fix" turns out not to be the problem: because parallel self-consistency calls cost essentially zero extra wall-clock latency versus a single call when issued concurrently, the three-call structure itself is very unlikely to be the dominant latency source — per-call output/input token volume is — and `candidateCount`, the API-native way to get multiple samples from one call, is reported broken on Gemini 3.x models specifically via multiple independent developer bug reports, so collapsing to fewer calls is not currently a viable native path regardless ([Google AI developer forum](https://discuss.ai.google.dev/t/massive-regression-candidatecount-1-multiple-candidates-disabled-in-gemini-3-flash-preview-and-newer-models/143068)). Batch mode (24-hour target turnaround) and model-level early-exit inference (a server-side technique not controllable by an API caller) are both real 2025-2026 techniques but not applicable to this system's interactive latency problem.

| Lever | Evidence strength | Expected impact | Action |
|---|---|---|---|
| Judge/arbiter aggregation over majority vote | Strong — controlled 42-task study | Likely meaningful accuracy gain | Test replacing majority vote with judge-selection on existing 3 calls |
| Output-token trimming (compact structured verdict) | Strong — production case study | ~40-79% latency reduction in analogous pipeline | Cut free-text rationale from each of the 3 calls |
| Context caching of shared prompt scaffold | Documented, supported on model; no numeric benchmark | Faster time-to-first-token | Cache schema/rubric/few-shot prefix across the 3 calls |
| Generalist+specialist hybrid by volatility/narrative axis | Moderate-strong — 66-year academic study | Likely narrows TSLA-style gap without regime-overfitting | Layer a specialist adjustment on the generalist base, not a pure per-stock model |
| Priority service tier | Documented by Google; no independent benchmark | Possible large latency cut at added cost | Load-test against ~68s baseline |
| Bull/Bear debate reasoning | Weak — no controlled accuracy comparison exists | Unknown | Treat as unproven; A/B test before adopting |
| Self-consistency sampling | Strong in general domain; untested for stock prediction; shrinking value on strong models | Unknown for this task | Measure empirically before investing |
| LLM + gradient-boosting ensemble | No direct evidence either way | Unknown — genuine white space | In-house experimentation only |
| Naive daily sentiment aggregation for TSLA-like stocks | Negative evidence (46% live accuracy, below chance) | Likely to hurt, not help, as currently conceived | Avoid blanket daily sentiment; consider event-triggered signals only |

## Conclusion

The two viral tweets and the credible-systems survey point at the same underlying truth from different directions: public claims of extraordinary AI trading performance are, without exception in everything checked here, either unverifiable narrative content or measurement artifacts (memorization, leakage, small non-rolling samples, or outright screenshot fabrication) rather than demonstrated breakthroughs, and the 55-66% ceiling remains the realistic frontier for genuine out-of-sample stock-direction prediction heading into 2026. That reframes the engineering question productively: this system's ~54% overall direction accuracy is not behind some hidden industry standard it needs to chase, and resources are better spent on the specific, evidence-backed levers identified here — judge-based call aggregation instead of majority voting, output-token-focused prompting, context caching, and a generalist-plus-specialist hybrid targeted at the narrative-stock weak spot — than on trying to replicate the structure of claims (debate theater, exotic alternative data, elaborate multi-agent personas) that the literature shows are either unproven or already debunked when examined closely. The most consequential open gap from this research is also the most actionable one: no study anywhere found tests whether any proposed accuracy fix reduces *correlated* error across the three parallel Gemini calls specifically, which means the team's own controlled A/B measurement — not another round of literature review — is now the highest-value next step.
