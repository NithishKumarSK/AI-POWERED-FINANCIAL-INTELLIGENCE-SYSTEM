# Credible Systems Survey 2025-2026: Does Anything Real Beat the ~55-66% Directional-Accuracy Ceiling?

## Are there any 2025-2026 peer-reviewed papers reporting stock/crypto direction prediction accuracy above 66%, with disclosed leakage controls and genuinely out-of-sample testing?

### Takeaway
One genuinely peer-reviewed 2025 paper (PLOS Complex Systems, 82% on SPY) claims to clear the ceiling but its abstract discloses no leakage/train-test methodology detail; the most methodologically transparent 2026 candidate (72% directional accuracy, chronological split, explicit leakage controls) is an **unreviewed arXiv preprint** merely "submitted" to a journal, built on only 20 stocks with a single fixed split rather than walk-forward testing. No 2025-2026 paper was found that combines (a) accuracy clearly above 66%, (b) full peer review, AND (c) fully disclosed, genuinely leak-proof out-of-sample testing all at once — every above-ceiling figure found has at least one of those three legs missing or unverifiable from available text.

### Cited Findings
- Cao, Chen, Lian & Zhang, "Volatility-informed SPY forecasting: From CGR-SPY analysis to DLSTM prediction," **PLOS Complex Systems**, vol. 2(8), Aug 2025, DOI 10.1371/journal.pcsy.0000037 — reports a Deep LSTM (DLSTM) "achieves 82% directional accuracy on daily SPY forecasts," pairing a copper-gold-ratio (CGR) signal for high-volatility regimes with the DLSTM for calmer regimes, validated with a simulated 1- and 3-month intraday trading test, outperforming a baseline LSTM and a binary classifier. — [IDEAS/RePEc listing](https://ideas.repec.org/a/plo/pcsy00/0000037.html); [Bohrium paper page](https://www.bohrium.com/paper-details/volatility-informed-spy-forecasting-from-cgr-spy-analysis-to-dlstm-prediction/1159762225318068226-135140)
- The abstract page for the above paper gives **no detail on train/test split, evaluation window, or look-ahead-bias safeguards** — those specifics, if present, are only in the full paywalled/full-text paper, which was not accessible via the pages checked. PLOS Complex Systems is a Public Library of Science journal (implying standard PLOS peer review), but the review process was not independently confirmed from the pages retrieved. — [IDEAS/RePEc listing](https://ideas.repec.org/a/plo/pcsy00/0000037.html)
- Al Ridhawi, Haj Ali & Al Osman (University of Ottawa), "AE-NodeFormer + SAC" regime-aware dual-node-transformer + Soft Actor-Critic model, arXiv 2603.19136v2 (2 Apr 2026) — reports **72% directional accuracy at the 1-day horizon**, 67% at 5 days, 63% at 20 days, on 20 S&P 500 stocks (training period 1982-2010, validation 2011-2016, **test period 2017-2025**, Table III). — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.19136v2)
- That same paper **does disclose real leakage controls**: strict chronological train/val/test splits, expanding-window z-score normalization with statistics frozen from the training period, forward-fill applied only in validation/test, graph-edge priors computed from training data only, causal attention masking, and a reinforcement-learning state restricted to prior-day (known-at-open) metrics. It reports a naive "always up" baseline of ~54% average directional accuracy over the test period and uses Theil's U against a random-walk benchmark. However, evaluation uses a **single fixed split rather than walk-forward/rolling validation**, the universe is only 20 stocks, and the paper's own header states it is "submitted to Applied Intelligence (Springer) for possible publication" — i.e., **not yet peer-reviewed**. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.19136v2)
- A related, already-published paper from a similar research line — Al Ridhawi et al., node-transformer + BERT sentiment, arXiv 2603.05917, now published in **IEEE Access** (peer-reviewed) — reports **65% one-day directional accuracy** on 20 S&P 500 stocks. This is within the established 55-66% band, not above it, and was already known from prior research. — [EconPapers/IDEAS listing](https://ideas.repec.org/p/arx/papers/2603.05917.html)
- Walid Siala, Ahmed Khanfir & Mike Papadakis, "Impact of LLMs news Sentiment Analysis on Stock Price Movement Prediction," arXiv 2602.00086v3 (Feb 2026) — a secondary summary reported "DeBERTa... accuracy of 75%" and an ensemble reaching "about 80%." On direct retrieval, the paper's results section was not accessible (PDF extraction truncated), so **it could not be confirmed whether these figures measure stock-direction accuracy or merely upstream news-sentiment classification accuracy** — the title suggests the former is the ultimate target, but the specific reported numbers may be the latter. No confirmed peer-reviewed venue was found (it is an arXiv posting; an earlier search suggested a possible ICLR 2026 workshop connection but this was not independently verified). — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.00086v3)
- As a negative/baseline data point reinforcing the ceiling: "Re(Visiting) Time Series Foundation Models in Finance," arXiv 2511.18578 (Nov 2025) — reports Chronos directional accuracy "just above 51%," with TimesFM just below that — i.e., general-purpose foundation models are barely better than a coin flip on stock direction. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.18578v1)
- As a red-flag example of a clearly inflated/likely-leaked figure: a 2025 Atlantis Press (ICIAAI 2025) conference proceedings paper reports an SVM "accuracy rate (92.48%)" for a stock-prediction comparative study — this venue is a low-selectivity conference-proceedings publisher, and a 92%+ figure in this literature is a strong leakage/overfitting red flag consistent with the "Profit Mirage" pattern rather than a credible result. — [Atlantis Press](https://www.atlantis-press.com/proceedings/iciaai-25/126015330)
- An engrxiv preprint (FinBERT + XGBoost on DJIA) reported "perfect classification," which **the authors themselves flagged as suggesting potential overfitting or data leakage** — a rare case of a paper self-disclosing the leakage problem rather than hiding it. — [engrxiv](https://engrxiv.org/preprint/download/5277/8907)

### Inferences
- The pattern across 2025-2026 papers is consistent with prior-year findings: as reported accuracy rises well above ~66%, the odds that the paper either (a) is not peer-reviewed, (b) uses a small/narrow universe and a single non-rolling split, (c) doesn't clearly specify leakage controls, or (d) is measuring a different metric than true next-day stock direction, all rise sharply. No single paper found cleanly satisfies all three of: above-66%, full peer review, and fully disclosed/robust OOS leakage controls.
- The field is moving toward disclosing leakage/memorization risk explicitly (chronological splits, LAP-style recall measures, FinLake-Bench) rather than resolving it — i.e., the center of gravity in 2026 is methodological skepticism about inflated figures, not new verified breakthroughs above the ceiling.

### Gaps
- Full text of the PLOS Complex Systems DLSTM paper (82% SPY accuracy) was not retrievable from the sources checked — its train/test methodology and leakage controls remain unverified. This is the single most important unresolved item: if it genuinely lacks leakage and is truly peer-reviewed, it would be the strongest above-ceiling candidate found.
- Whether arXiv 2602.00086's 75-80% figures measure stock-direction accuracy vs. sentiment-classification accuracy was not resolved from available text.
- Could not independently verify the final/published, peer-reviewed status (vs. preprint) of arXiv 2603.19136 (72% accuracy) — it had not yet appeared as a confirmed Applied Intelligence publication as of the search date.

## Additional relevant finding: 2025-2026 research directly extends the "Profit Mirage" memorization-leakage finding

### Takeaway
Multiple new 2025-2026 papers (independent of the original "Profit Mirage" authors) converge on the same conclusion using different methods: LLM-based financial forecasts' apparent predictive power is substantially inflated by training-data memorization, and this inflation collapses on genuinely post-cutoff data — strengthening rather than undermining the "Profit Mirage" finding and further explaining why above-ceiling claims should be viewed skeptically.

### Cited Findings
- Zhenyu Gao, Wenxi Jiang & Yutong Yan (CUHK Business School), "A Test of Lookahead Bias in LLM Forecasts," arXiv 2512.23847 (drafted Dec 2025, revised June 2026) — using a "Lookahead-Avoidance Probability" (LAP) measure of how likely a model is to recall a given firm-date outcome, find the memorization-linked component adds about 0.077 percentage points to the marginal effect of an LLM prediction on next-day stock return, roughly **37% of the standalone LLM effect** in their baseline test; the interaction (and thus the apparent edge) **loses statistical significance on samples after the model's training cutoff**. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.23847v1); discussed at [ABFER 2026](https://abfer.org/media/abfer-events-2026/annual-conference/slides-tech-ai/260518-AI-A-Test-of-Lookahead-Bias-in-LLM-Forecasts.pdf)
- This builds directly on an earlier Lopez-Lira-coauthored paper, "The Memorization Problem" (arXiv 2504.14765), which documented that LLMs can recall economic/stock data and that apparent forecasting success may reflect memorization rather than insight. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.14765v2)
- "MemGuard-Alpha" (arXiv 2603.26797, March 2026) proposes a mitigation: filtering out memorization-contaminated signals before they reach a trading strategy — an indication the field is now building defensive tooling around this problem rather than claiming it's solved. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.26797.pdf)
- A separate, more positive-sounding 2026 paper, Chen & Pu, "Agentic AI nowcasting" (arXiv 2601.11958, Jan 2026), claims a **genuinely live, real-time, out-of-sample-by-construction** test (predictions collected daily starting April 2025, with the LLM searching the live web rather than being fed curated text) and finds a real, if narrow, edge: longing the 20 highest-ranked Russell 1000 stocks yields an 18.4 bps/day Fama-French-five-factor-plus-momentum alpha and 2.43 annualized Sharpe (after costs), but **bottom-ranked stocks show no edge and alpha dilutes quickly beyond the top tier**. Critically, this paper reports **alpha and Sharpe, not a hit-rate/accuracy percentage**, so it cannot be used to claim an above-55-66%-ceiling direction-accuracy figure — it's a different (and arguably more rigorous, since truly live/forward-tested) kind of claim entirely. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.11958)
- Lehner & Lopez-Lira, "ChatGPT as a Time Capsule: The Limits of Price Discovery," arXiv 2604.21433 (2026) — a direct follow-up to the original Lopez-Lira & Tang GPT-4 study, using 12 OpenAI model snapshots (2021-2025) as time-stamped text summaries; finds a sector-neutral "outlook score" is positively associated with future analyst revisions, target-price changes, and one-month returns, with predictability generally increasing with horizon; interprets the result as evidence of a cost-of-information-aggregation bottleneck rather than investor inattention. The abstract reports **no accuracy percentage** (only a t-statistic of 6.02 for the key association), and whether/how the authors control for memorization was not visible in the abstract alone. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.21433)

### Inferences
- The most methodologically careful 2025-2026 work (Gao/Jiang/Yan; Profit Mirage; the live-forward Chen & Pu study) is converging on a nuanced but consistent picture: genuine, non-memorization-driven predictive signal exists but is modest (alpha/Sharpe-sized, concentrated in a small top-ranked tier, or within the known 55-66% accuracy band) — not a validated breakthrough clearing the ~66% ceiling on raw next-day direction accuracy.
- Papers reporting high accuracy (72%, 75-82%) tend to either lack peer review, use narrow/small-universe single-split designs, leave leakage controls unstated, or measure something other than pure stock-direction accuracy — none simultaneously satisfies rigor on all fronts.

### Gaps
- Full text of Lehner & Lopez-Lira (2604.21433) was not read beyond the abstract; its handling of memorization specifically was not confirmed.
- Could not determine whether the Gao/Jiang/Yan LAP methodology has been applied yet to any of the above-ceiling 2025-2026 accuracy papers (e.g., the PLOS 82% or arXiv 72% results) to test whether their edges would also collapse — this would be the most direct way to stress-test those specific above-ceiling claims, but no such cross-study test was found.

## Overall honest verdict

### Takeaway
As of November 2026, **no new system or paper was found that credibly, robustly clears the ~55-66% academic ceiling for short-to-medium-horizon stock/crypto direction prediction**. The closest candidates (PLOS Complex Systems' 82% SPY claim; the 72% arXiv preprint with disclosed-but-non-rolling leakage controls) are each missing at least one leg of genuine credibility (full methodology transparency, peer review, or robust walk-forward/rolling out-of-sample design and reasonable universe size), and the single most methodologically rigorous *live, forward, out-of-sample* 2026 test found (Chen & Pu's agentic nowcasting) reports alpha/Sharpe, not a hit-rate figure, and shows the edge is concentrated and narrow rather than a general >66% directional accuracy. Meanwhile, a growing and increasingly sophisticated 2025-2026 literature (Gao/Jiang/Yan's lookahead-bias test, MemGuard-Alpha) is actively reinforcing, with new methods, the "Profit Mirage" conclusion that apparent above-ceiling edges in LLM-based systems are substantially memorization artifacts. No named hedge fund, prop firm, or fintech was found to have disclosed an audited hit-rate (as opposed to a returns/Sharpe figure) in 2025-2026, and no Kaggle competition or benchmark produced a verifiable winning accuracy figure for short-horizon stock direction.
