## What is the EXACT experimental design (instant reaction vs. forward-looking tradable test)?

### Takeaway
The paper has one data pipeline (headline + GPT score) feeding two distinct measurement windows: an "initial reaction" window (close-to-open, or close-to-15-minutes-post-release) that is explicitly framed as non-tradable because it requires trading before/at the public release of news, and a "forward-looking drift" window (open-to-close, or post-15-minutes-to-close-to-next-close) that is the actually implementable, tradable strategy and is the one subjected to transaction-cost and robustness analysis.

### Cited Findings
- Sample: headlines for 4,123 U.S. common stocks from October 2021 to May 2024, totaling 159,137 firm-headline-date observations, collected by web-scraping and matched to RavenPack — [arXiv HTML full text v6](https://arxiv.org/html/2304.07619v6)
- Filtering: headlines required a RavenPack relevance score of 100; only complete articles and press releases were kept (not snippets); "stock-gain"/"stock-loss" mechanical headline types were excluded; event-similarity-days filter above 90; near-duplicate headlines removed via a similarity threshold of 0.6 — [arXiv HTML full text v6](https://arxiv.org/html/2304.07619v6)
- Composition: about 67.5% of headlines are press releases and 32.5% are news articles; about 82% (129,953 headlines) are "overnight" news released before 9am or after 4pm, and about 18% are "intraday" news released during trading hours — [arXiv HTML full text v6](https://arxiv.org/html/2304.07619v6)
- Model/prompt: GPT-4 snapshot "gpt-4-0314" at temperature 0; the prompt instructs the model to act as a financial expert and answer YES/NO/UNKNOWN with one sentence of reasoning; YES=1, UNKNOWN=0, NO=-1; mean score across the sample is 0.31 (skewed positive) — [arXiv HTML full text v6](https://arxiv.org/html/2304.07619v6)
- **Initial/instant reaction test (framed by the authors as NOT tradable by the public):** For overnight news, measured as the return from the prior day's close to the next open; for intraday news, measured as the return from the prior close to 15 minutes after the headline's release. Long-short portfolios go long GPT-4-positive-scored firms and short GPT-4-negative-scored firms within this window — [arXiv HTML full text v6](https://arxiv.org/html/2304.07619v6)
- **Forward-looking/drift test (the tradable strategy):** For overnight news released before 9am, the strategy enters at that day's open and exits at that day's close; news released after the prior close enters at the next open and exits at the next close (daily rebalancing). For intraday news, three separate windows are analyzed: (1) 1 to 15 minutes post-release, (2) 15 minutes post-release to the news-day close, and (3) the news-day close to the next day's close — [arXiv HTML full text v6](https://arxiv.org/html/2304.07619v6)
- Econometric specification: headline-level panel regressions of returns on the GPT score with firm and date fixed effects, r = a_i + b_t + γ′x + ε, with standard errors double-clustered by date and firm — [arXiv HTML full text v6](https://arxiv.org/html/2304.07619v6)
- The 1-to-15-minute intraday window specifically shows no significant predictability, consistent with that window being too close to the reaction/non-tradable boundary — [arXiv HTML full text v6](https://arxiv.org/html/2304.07619v6)
- Holding-period/decay-of-signal test: the authors extend the long-short GPT-4 strategy to a 5-trading-day hold. For overnight news, returns are 34 bps on day 0 and 19 bps on day 1 (then insignificant); for intraday news, 32 bps and 30 bps on days 0-1 (then insignificant). The authors conclude: "information contained in the firm-specific news in our sample is absorbed into market prices in about two days." — [arXiv HTML full text v6](https://arxiv.org/html/2304.07619v6)
- Size/news-type heterogeneity: large-cap stocks show no post-negative-news drift, while small-cap stocks continue to drift for longer — read by the authors as direct evidence that large-cap markets are more efficient/faster to arbitrage away the signal — [arXiv HTML full text v6](https://arxiv.org/html/2304.07619v6)
- Per earlier CNBC coverage of an early version of the study, the original pilot used ChatGPT-3.5 (not GPT-4) on "over 50,000 headlines" about NYSE/Nasdaq/small-cap-exchange stocks starting October 2022, classifying each as good/bad/uncertain for the stock and comparing to next-day returns — this description matches an earlier (pre-GPT-4, pre-RavenPack-159k-sample) version of the project and should be read as describing the v1/v2 pilot design, not the final v6 design summarized above — [CNBC, April 2023](https://www.cnbc.com/2023/04/12/chatgpt-may-be-able-to-predict-stock-movements-finance-professor-says.html)

### Inferences
- The "93.3% / 88.8%" instant-reaction numbers and the "55-58%" forward numbers in the user's brief map directly onto this paper's own reported hit rates: 93.3% (overnight initial reaction), 88.8% (intraday initial reaction), 58% (overnight drift/forward), 55% (intraday drift/forward) — see the full table in the next section.
- The clean separation between "reaction" (close-to-open or close-to-+15min, requires trading before normal investors can react) and "drift" (open-to-close, fully tradable after public release) is what makes this paper's forward-looking number far more credible than many competing LLM-stock-prediction studies that conflate same-day/overlapping windows.

## Full table of results and Sharpe ratios across time periods, and WHY the edge decayed

### Takeaway
The paper discloses exactly four period-level Sharpe ratios for the GPT-4 overnight drift strategy — 6.54 (2021 Q4), 3.68 (2022), 2.33 (2023), and 1.22 (January-May 2024) — with no finer monthly/quarterly breakdown in the main text; the authors' own explanation is a price-impact/arbitrage mechanism (their formal Proposition 2/B.4: as more traders adopt capable LLMs and act on the same signal, prices converge to the LLM-implied valuation, shrinking the exploitable gap), while explicitly hedging that "other factors, such as changing market conditions, could also contribute" and stopping short of claiming the decay is strictly caused by LLM adoption.

### Cited Findings
- **Headline hit rates (initial reaction, non-tradable):** 93.3% for overnight news, 88.8% for intraday news; abstract rounds this to "approximately 90%"; by sub-category, GPT-4's initial hit rate ranges 89-91%, and is 91% for both news articles and press releases separately — [arXiv HTML full text v6](https://arxiv.org/html/2304.07619v6)
- **Headline hit rates (forward drift, tradable):** 58% overnight, 55% intraday; by sub-category the range is 55-59% — [arXiv HTML full text v6](https://arxiv.org/html/2304.07619v6)
- Comparator model hit rates — initial reaction / drift: GPT-3.5 93% / 56%; DistilBart-MNLI 87% / 56%; BART-Large and Llama2-70b ~86% initial; FinBERT 90% initial but only 48% drift (and FinBERT only makes a directional call on ~25% of overnight news); GPT-1, GPT-2, and BERT all below 65% initial hit rate; OpenAI embeddings 84.6% initial (overnight), MiniLM embeddings 80.2% initial (overnight) — [arXiv HTML full text v6](https://arxiv.org/html/2304.07619v6)
- **Sharpe ratios, overnight drift strategy, full model table:** GPT-4 = 2.97 (intraday drift: 2.63); GPT-3.5 = 1.66; DistilBart-MNLI = 1.26; BART-Large = 1.05; Llama2-70b = 0.97; "most basic models" = negative; OpenAI embeddings = 2.53 overnight / 1.71 intraday; MiniLM embeddings = 1.42 overnight / 0.79 intraday — [arXiv HTML full text v6](https://arxiv.org/html/2304.07619v6)
- GPT-4 Sharpe by news category ranges 1.47 to 2.32; news articles specifically 2.23; press releases specifically 1.96 — [arXiv HTML full text v6](https://arxiv.org/html/2304.07619v6)
- Overnight drift strategy decomposed by leg: long leg averages 8 bps/day with Sharpe 0.78; short leg averages 26 bps/day with Sharpe 2.01 (i.e., most of the edge is on the short/negative-news side) — [arXiv HTML full text v6](https://arxiv.org/html/2304.07619v6)
- **Time-period Sharpe decay (the key headline numbers), overnight GPT-4 drift strategy, annualized:** 2021 Q4 = 6.54; 2022 = 3.68; 2023 = 2.33; January-May 2024 = 1.22. The HTML full text explicitly states "The excerpt gives no monthly or further year-by-year breakdown. The only numbers are the four period values above" — confirmed on a second, deeper fetch of the same section — [arXiv HTML full text v6, Section 8](https://arxiv.org/html/2304.07619v6)
- A close-to-close (less-timing-sensitive) version of the strategy "remains significantly positive in 2022 and 2023, but becomes insignificant in January-May 2024," per Figure OA2 in the Online Appendix (figure itself not captured in the text extraction) — [arXiv HTML full text v6, Section 8](https://arxiv.org/html/2304.07619v6)
- **Authors' own explanation for the decay:** "LLMs can increase investors' information processing capacity and reduce market inefficiencies." The authors describe the Sharpe decline as "suggestive evidence" that "AI adoption is actively reducing information processing frictions," explicitly tied to a formal price-impact channel (Proposition 2 in the theoretical model): as more traders act on the same LLM signal, prices move toward the LLM's valuation, shrinking the profit-generating gap — [arXiv HTML full text v6]
- Critically, the authors hedge this causal claim: "other factors, such as changing market conditions, could also contribute" — the paper does NOT claim to have isolated LLM-adoption as the sole or even dominant cause of the decay; it offers the LLM-adoption/efficiency story as the consistent, model-backed interpretation but acknowledges it has not ruled out alternative explanations (e.g., general market conditions) — [arXiv HTML full text v6, Section 8]
- Separately, in a 2023 CNBC interview Lopez-Lira personally speculated more aggressively than the paper's formal hedge: he expects predictability to fall as institutions adopt these tools, estimating "by the year five, there will be zero return predictability," and separately noted the study only measured next-day returns while in practice markets may price news within seconds — [CNBC, April 2023](https://www.cnbc.com/2023/04/12/chatgpt-may-be-able-to-predict-stock-movements-finance-professor-says.html)
- Transaction-cost sensitivity of the (tradable) overnight drift strategy: at 5 bps round-trip cost, cumulative return stays above 300%; at 10 bps, above 100%; at 20 bps round-trip, the strategy becomes unprofitable — directly matching the user's brief — [arXiv HTML full text v6]
- Partial-rebalancing mitigation: at 10bps cost, 100% daily rebalancing gives Sharpe 1.29 while rebalancing only 25% of the portfolio daily gives Sharpe 1.34, cutting turnover from ~190%/day to ~46%/day — i.e., the authors show a way to partially offset transaction-cost drag by rebalancing less aggressively — [arXiv HTML full text v6]

### Inferences
- Because only four period-aggregated Sharpe ratios are disclosed (not monthly/quarterly), any claim of a smooth, monotonic decay trajectory beyond those four anchor points is not directly supported by the paper's disclosed numbers — the actual within-period path (e.g., within 2022 or within 2023) is unknown from the text extracted.

## Why is GPT-4's edge real/tradable despite being "mere" pattern recognition over text? Memorization/leakage safeguards

### Takeaway
The paper's answer is a formal limited-attention/limits-to-arbitrage model: GPT-4 is a cheap substitute for the costly human attention needed to fully process news in real time, so its score approximates the "fully-processed" post-news valuation that inattentive investors only reach with delay — this gap is real and tradable precisely because most market participants are not yet using LLMs this way, and it is self-erasing by construction (their own proposition states predictability "vanishes" once enough inattentive agents adopt comparably capable LLMs). On leakage, the paper's primary safeguard is a strict post-training-cutoff sample start date, but the authors' own later paper shows that cutoff-based sampling alone is an incomplete safeguard against memorization.

### Cited Findings
- Core theoretical framework (Appendix B): the model has attentive agents (who process most news quickly), inattentive agents (who process only part of the news and update with delay), and noise traders (who create limits to arbitrage) with CARA utility — [arXiv HTML full text v6]
- Mechanism: the LLM's assessment is modeled as a better estimate of post-news fundamental value than the current price; as inattentive agents (or algorithms trading on their behalf) incorporate the LLM signal, price drifts from the initial (partially informed) level toward the more fully-processed level — [arXiv HTML full text v6]
- **Theorem 1 / Theorem B.1:** there exists a sophistication threshold k* such that "only LLMs with k > k* can predict returns profitably on average" — i.e., the model formally predicts that weak/small LLMs (consistent with the empirical finding that GPT-1, GPT-2, and BERT score below 65% hit rate) should NOT generate tradable edge, while sufficiently capable models (GPT-3.5, GPT-4) should — [arXiv HTML full text v6]
- **Proposition 1 / B.2:** predictability should be larger in small, illiquid stocks and after negative news — directly matched empirically (large-caps show no post-negative-news drift; small-caps do; the short/negative-news leg carries most of the Sharpe ratio: 26bps/day, Sharpe 2.01, versus the long leg's 8bps/day, Sharpe 0.78) — [arXiv HTML full text v6]
- **Proposition 2 / B.4:** predictability erodes as high-quality LLMs spread among market participants, through a price-impact channel; if all inattentive agents adopt LLMs with capacity close to that of attentive agents, return predictability "potentially vanish[es]" — this is the formal backbone for the empirically observed Sharpe decay (6.54 to 1.22) — [arXiv HTML full text v6]
- **Theorem 2 / Proposition B.5:** if attentive agents themselves adopt LLMs exceeding their own information-processing capacity, overall market efficiency improves as model size increases — a complementary mechanism by which widespread "good" LLM adoption is net positive for price discovery, not just edge-destroying for traders — [arXiv HTML full text v6]
- The authors' own framing of the mechanism, verbatim via their Section 9 conclusion: "we find suggestive evidence that widespread LLM adoption can enhance price efficiency," and elsewhere the paper's thesis is paraphrased as "the LLM's predictive success inherently contains the seeds of its own obsolescence as a trading signal" — [arXiv HTML full text v6]
- **Training-cutoff/leakage safeguard — confirmed design choice:** the GPT-4 snapshot used is "gpt-4-0314," whose training data runs through September 2021; the study's headline sample begins in October 2021 — i.e., deliberately starting the test sample in the month immediately after the model's training cutoff — [arXiv HTML full text v6]
- The authors cite prior methodological literature establishing the principle that evaluation must use strictly post-cutoff data to avoid lookahead bias/memorization (citing Sarkar & Vafa 2024, "Lookahead Bias in Pretrained Language Models," among others) — [arXiv HTML full text v6]
- A specific internal check: in a footnote, the authors report comparing GPT-4 versus GPT-3.5 performance over time in an Online Appendix, finding "GPT-4 performs better in the latter part of the sample," which they argue is inconsistent with the results being driven by pre-cutoff knowledge/memorization (since GPT-4's cutoff is earlier in the sample timeline than later sample dates, performance should not systematically improve later if memorization were driving results) — [arXiv HTML full text v6]
- Llama-2's training cutoff is later (September 2022) than GPT-4's; the authors explicitly flag that cross-model-family comparisons "may be overstated" in predictive power because of this cutoff mismatch — a notable instance of the authors proactively flagging their own potential leakage confound — [arXiv HTML full text v6]
- Model snapshots used are fixed/pinned and do not receive updates during the study — ruling out silent model drift as a confound — [arXiv HTML full text v6]
- **However, the authors' own later, more skeptical paper ("The Memorization Problem," 2025) directly undercuts the sufficiency of a simple post-cutoff-start-date safeguard:** it shows GPT-4o can reproduce pre-cutoff economic data (S&P 500 levels, unemployment rates to within 0.1 percentage point, GDP figures) from memory, that explicit prompting to "respect historical boundaries" does not prevent recall-level accuracy, that masking names/dates does not prevent the model reconstructing entities/dates from context, and that small post-cutoff samples alone are "not a valid robustness check" since skill and undetected memorization are statistically indistinguishable in small samples — [arXiv 2504.14765](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.14765v2)
- The CUHK "Lookahead Propensity" paper's empirical finding (memorization channel explaining ~37% of the standalone LLM effect in a news-headline/next-day-return test) is a direct, if indirect, test of exactly the kind of design the Lopez-Lira/Tang paper uses — though it was not confirmed whether this CUHK result was run specifically on the Lopez-Lira/Tang dataset or GPT-4-0314 model, versus a different/generic LLM-forecast setup — [arXiv HTML 2512.23847](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.23847v1)

### Inferences
- There is an internal tension in the authors' own body of work: the 2023-2025 ChatGPT-stock-prediction paper relies primarily on "start the sample right after the training cutoff" as its leakage safeguard, while the same authors' 2025 "Memorization Problem" paper argues that this exact safeguard is insufficient on its own for small samples. This suggests the Lopez-Lira/Tang headline paper's leakage-robustness should be regarded as reasonably strong (159K observations is not a "small sample," and the GPT-4-vs-GPT-3.5 cross-time-performance footnote is a genuine, if indirect, falsification check) but not airtight by the standard the authors themselves later established — a team wanting to "exceed" this work should adopt the more rigorous anonymization/counterfactual-logic techniques proposed in the authors' own follow-up paper rather than relying on cutoff-date sampling alone.
- The formal theoretical model (limited attention + limits to arbitrage + LLM-capacity threshold) is likely the single biggest reason this paper is regarded as "rigorous" relative to competing atheoretical LLM-stock-picking studies: it generates specific, falsifiable, and empirically-matched cross-sectional predictions (small-cap > large-cap effect size; negative-news > positive-news effect size; weak-LLM no-effect vs. strong-LLM effect; adoption-driven decay) rather than simply reporting a single backtest Sharpe ratio.

### Gaps
- The exact mathematical proofs/derivations underlying Theorem 1, Proposition 2, Theorem 2, and Proposition B.5 are in a Section D Online Appendix that was not retrievable via the HTML text extraction tool used (content was referenced but not displayed in the fetched text).
- Could not confirm whether the CUHK LAP paper's 37%-memorization-channel finding was computed using the Lopez-Lira/Tang paper's own replication setup or an independently constructed dataset — this materially affects how directly that finding should be read as a critique of this specific paper versus the broader literature.

## Limitations, robustness checks, and out-of-sample validation (beyond what was already known)

### Takeaway
Beyond the headline Sharpe-decay and 20bps-breakeven numbers, the paper reports several additional, previously-undisclosed-to-the-task-brief robustness checks: prompt-order invariance, a partial-rebalancing turnover/Sharpe tradeoff, robustness to excluding penny/micro-cap stocks, low factor-model loadings, and several explicit, self-acknowledged limitations about implementability and sample selection.

### Cited Findings
- **Prompt-order robustness:** on a random sample of 1,000 headlines, asking the model to give its answer first versus its reasoning first produced "very similar recommendations" — ruling out prompt-ordering as a confound — [arXiv HTML full text v6]
- **Partial rebalancing:** at a 10bps round-trip cost, 100% daily rebalancing yields Sharpe 1.29 with turnover ~190%/day, while rebalancing only 25% of the portfolio daily yields a higher Sharpe of 1.34 with turnover cut to ~46%/day — showing a practical mitigation for transaction-cost drag that a team could implement directly — [arXiv HTML full text v6]
- **Small/illiquid-stock robustness:** removing stocks priced at or below $5 and those below the 20th NYSE size percentile still leaves cumulative strategy returns above 300% — i.e., the edge is not solely a penny-stock/microstructure artifact, though (per Proposition 1) it is concentrated in smaller names — [arXiv HTML full text v6]
- **Factor exposure:** five-factor-model (presumably Fama-French-style) alphas are close in magnitude to the strategy's raw average daily returns, meaning the strategy has low loadings on standard risk factors — the edge is not simply repackaged factor exposure — [arXiv HTML full text v6]
- **Embeddings as a deliberately conservative lower bound:** the authors explicitly call their text-embedding-based comparison results (OpenAI/MiniLM embeddings with logistic regression) "a lower bound" on what's achievable, since they used default/unoptimized settings (max_iter=10) rather than tuning the embedding-classifier pipeline — a flagged limitation of their own benchmark comparisons — [arXiv HTML full text v6]
- **Explicit author-acknowledged limitations:**
  - The trading strategies are described as "not optimized for implementation" — the authors state their intent is to show "raw forecasting power," not a production-ready strategy — [arXiv HTML full text v6]
  - Profitability is cost-sensitive (breaks at 20bps round-trip), which the authors say is "challenging for large or unsophisticated investors" to achieve in practice — [arXiv HTML full text v6]
  - GPT-3.5's strong drift return is noted to come largely from a short leg of only about six stocks per day — a thin, concentration-risk-laden result that the authors flag themselves — [arXiv HTML full text v6]
  - GPT-3.5 and GPT-4 parameter counts used in the "model sophistication" framing are described as "unconfirmed estimates" (since OpenAI does not officially disclose them) — [arXiv HTML full text v6]
  - The matched-firm sample (headline-covered stocks) is on average larger and more liquid than the full CRSP universe, i.e., a selected, not fully representative, sample — [arXiv HTML full text v6]
  - The decay evidence for the adoption/efficiency story is explicitly self-described as "suggestive," not causal — [arXiv HTML full text v6]
- **Out-of-sample framing:** the entire study's core out-of-sample claim is that all tested headlines (Oct 2021 onward) post-date GPT-4's training cutoff (Sept 2021), which the authors treat as their primary out-of-sample/no-leakage guarantee, supplemented by the GPT-4-vs-GPT-3.5-over-time footnote check described above — [arXiv HTML full text v6]

### Inferences
- The combination of "not optimized for implementation" plus the 20bps breakeven plus the partial-rebalancing finding (Sharpe 1.34 at 25% rebalancing, lower turnover) suggests that a team trying to build a comparable but actually tradable system should prioritize (a) reducing turnover via partial/threshold rebalancing rather than daily full rebalancing, and (b) focusing capital on the short/negative-news leg, which the paper shows carries most of the statistically reliable edge (Sharpe 2.01 vs. 0.78 for the long leg).
